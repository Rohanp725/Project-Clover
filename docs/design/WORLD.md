# The world, its gods and the story behind them

Notes from the think sessions between the user and Claude on 2026-11-16 and 17 (the afternoon session of 2026-11-17
was held in chat, outside the code session, and merged here the same day). Each item carries a status, as in
`docs/GAME.md`: **Decided** (the user said so), **Leaning** (the user's current preference), **Proposal** (Claude's
idea, not yet accepted), **Open**. Where this file and `docs/GAME.md` differ, this file is newer; the two are to be
brought together once the user has confirmed the open points.

## How the world is laid out

- **Leaning (user)**: each area is its own themed land, drawn from one region's mythology. After a god falls the
  player boards a themed means of travel and the game cuts forward to the next land. The ride belongs to the land
  being left, so it still works when the order of gods changes inside a tier.
- **Decided (user)**: every godly second form is a mythological or godly creature.
- **Decided (user)**: each god's name is an original name with the sound and feel of its source, a nod and never the
  deity's own name.
- **Decided (user)**: some duo fights stay, reworked into pairs that make sense in myth.

- **Decided (user, 2026-11-17)**: the first land is Fire's, the sun-king's city. The player starts there with fists
  and no element, and takes the first element from the Fire god. This replaces the earlier decision in
  `docs/GAME.md` that the first run starts with fists and one chosen element.
- **Proposal**: because the player has nothing to grow yet, the first god is simply taken from; the take-or-consume
  choice first appears at the second god.
- **Decided (user, 2026-11-17)**: after the demo, the ordinary enemies and the area of the Lightning land are fully
  reworked to fit its theme much better.
- **Liked as first drafts (user, 2026-11-17)**: the sketches of each land's feel, walk, god's room and ride that
  Claude gave in the session (sun-king's city, fished-up islands, huntress's fjord, smith's foundry, alchemist's
  court, the stones and the grove, the seat under the eclipse, and the last fight in the hub). To be written out
  here land by land as each is worked through.

## Rules that hold for the whole game (2026-11-17, afternoon)

### Every god transforms, and the choice is made over the body

- **Decided (user)**: no god's human body simply dies before the godly form. The god transforms into the form in
  front of the player, and when the form is beaten it turns back into the human, who falls. The take-or-consume
  choice is made over that human body.
- **Decided (user)**, the user's example for Lightning: Narukami, beaten, takes a knee; lightning strikes him; he
  becomes the bolt; the camera pulls back to show the storm dragon. When the dragon falls, lightning strikes once
  more, he reappears and falls over, and his body is where the choice is made.
- **Decided (user)**: in the few seconds back in human form, each god is sane, and says one very short line of
  regret. Only the last champion has time to say more (see his land).
- **Decided (user)**: the regret lines below stay as written. They do not need to hint at the god murdered before.
  - Narukami (Lightning): "I only wanted the storm to answer me."
  - Kazehaya (Wind): "I was always first. There was never anyone at the end." (Decided, user, 2026-11-18)
  - Khepharu (Fire): "I promised them gold. I gave them fire."
  - The fisher (Water): "I pulled up islands... and drowned them."
  - The huntress (Ice): "The hunt never ended. I forgot what I hunted."
  - The giant (Earth): "I raised the stones so they'd remember me."
  - The antlered lord (Life): "I let the forest grow through them."
  - The smith (Metal): "I built a body that wouldn't limp. I never left it."
  - The alchemist (Poison): "So this is what forever tastes like."
- The transformation for each god is listed in that god's section. Earth and Life: see their land below.
- **Decided (user, 2026-11-17, night)**: in a duo land the take-or-consume choice is made over each body separately.
  The same rule holds for Lightning and Wind.

### The upgrade currency is Shards

- **Decided (user)**: "embers" is renamed **Shards**, because embers suggests fire and the game has many elements.
  Everywhere this file, `docs/GAME.md` and the Fire land's design notes say embers, read Shards. (The built game
  still says Embers until it is renamed.)
- **Proposal**: Shards are the pieces of the broken elements, which ties them to the breaking. On a fall the carried
  Shards scatter, and the enemies who held them pick them back up (the rule decided for the Fire land below).

### The reflect arc

- **Decided (user)**: the sun-parry from the Fire land (a perfect guard against the sun) is built for real, not
  kept as a maybe. It pays off in the last two fights.
- **Decided (user)**: each reflect in the game has its own feel, but each is similar enough to the later ones that
  players build up experience for them.
- **Proposal**, the order of the lessons:
  1. Fire: a single perfect guard against one huge thing, the sun.
  2. Ice: reflecting the huntress's arrows back at her; reading a volley for the one clean reflect.
  3. The champion: cracking the eclipse with reflected arrows, then guarding the world-ending arrow.
  4. The sponsor: the same reflect once more, on the one moment it should never have replayed.

### The order of the climb

- **Decided (user)**:
  - Tier 1: Fire (always first).
  - Tier 2: Water and Metal.
  - Tier 3: Ice and Poison. Poison is one of the last solo fights, if not the last, because it is the most
    chaotic.
  - Tier 4: the two duo lands, Lightning and Wind, and Earth and Life.
  - Then the land of the last champion (the seat under the eclipse), then the sponsor.

## Fire's land: the sun-king's city

- **Liked (user, 2026-11-17)**: four kinds of corruption by fire, of the six Claude offered. *The wick*: they burn
  and are not consumed; the wrapped dead smoulder like candle wicks and the living are charred with ember light in
  the cracks; they grab and hold to share the burning. *The gilded*: the sun-king promised his people the golden flesh
  of gods and it came true as molten gold, masks fused to faces; the more gold, the higher the rank. *Sun-blind*:
  they looked at their god until their eyes burned out, and hunt by heat and by shadow. *The thirst*: fire as
  drought; dried husks that crave anything wet and may go for the player's flasks.
- **Proposal**: the wick and the gilded are the land's ladder of rank (common people, then soldiers and priests,
  then the god as the sun); the sun-blind and the thirsting are single enemy types inside it. It is always noon there.
- **Leaning (user, 2026-11-17)**: lands need larger first areas to explore and to carry the land's lore, even if
  sparse in the manner of Elden Ring.
- **Proposal (Claude, 2026-11-17), the land in three districts, an elite and the god**:
  1. *The dry river*: where the player wakes. A dead riverbed, cracked mud, reed boats lying where the water left
     them, a harbour with no water. The thirsting. Fists only; the first rest fire. Landmark: a great barque stranded
     on the mud (no longer the ride out: see the changes of 2026-11-17 afternoon below). Side path: a sealed well
     shrine.
  2. *The city of wicks*: mud-brick streets and the embalmers' quarter. The wicks. Narrow alleys, grabbing hands;
     the plain spear is found at a gatehouse. Side path: painted tombs that show the star falling in the desert and a
     mortal carrying it home, and under the paint an older god of the hearth and the dawn.
  3. *The golden way*: the avenue of seated statues, their faces recut from the old god's to the king's, and the
     hall of columns before the court. The gilded, and the sun-blind in the columns' light and shade.
  - *Elite*: one of the wholly gilded, a champion cast in gold who holds the last gate with a spear and shows the
    god's patterns before the god does.
  - *The god's room*: an open sun-court with an obelisk whose shadow turns like a clock hand. First a spear duel
    with the sun-king as a man. Then the form: the scarab that rolls the sun across the court and the falcon that
    carries it up, one creature in two shapes, as the sun was a beetle at dawn and a falcon at noon. The sun is the
    shared piece of the fight. When the whole court burns, the obelisk's moving shadow is the only safe ground.
  - The player has no choice here but to take Fire. Then the ride out (now the sunset, below).
- **Built as a first pass (2026-11-17, unattended; not yet played or accepted by the user)**: the scenes `FireYard`
  (a yard to look at the four enemies), `SunCity` (the three districts, the side paths, the Champion of the Gate)
  and `SunCourt` (the god, here named Khepharu, a nod to the scarab of the morning sun; the name awaits the user's
  yes). None is in the playtest build. What was built, and where it departs from the specification, is in the three
  "As built" sections of `docs/design/fire_land.md`.
- **Decided (user, 2026-11-17)**: the name Khepharu stays. The sponsor's voice lines in the city stay.
- **Decided (user, 2026-11-17)**: unspent embers are lost on a fall, but the enemies the player had killed to earn
  them carry them again when they come back; killing those enemies again gives the embers back.
- **Leaning (user, 2026-11-17)**: shade is a rule of the whole land, not only of the god's court: there are times of
  high heat when the player must find shade or burn, which also tells of the land's corruption.
- **Leaning (user, 2026-11-17, morning)**: perhaps a parry made for the sun itself, a perfect guard that does
  something special to it. The user is not sure; build it as a proposal. **Superseded (afternoon)**: Decided, it is
  built for real and is the first lesson of the reflect arc above.
- **Decided (user, 2026-11-17)**: the first pass of the land is not good enough in two ways. Areas must be designed
  places, not flat square yards: shape, height, routes, sight lines. Enemies must fight in ways of their own and not
  be reskins of other enemies' attacks (the user's example: a burning ash enemy that bursts when the player is too
  close). The bosses' attacks need better visuals.
- **Built as a second pass (2026-11-17, unattended; not yet played or accepted by the user)**: the enemies' own
  mechanics, the city rebuilt from `docs/design/fire_levels.md`, the bosses' attack effects, the sun parry and the

## How a climb is structured

- **Leaning (user, 2026-11-17)**: the single run in the manner of Hades gives way to this, which the user called
  "B with C" of the options offered. Death returns the player to the last rest fire in the land they are in, in the
  manner of Elden Ring and Sekiro: the sponsor turns time back a little, not all the way. Gods already beaten stay
  beaten and the build is kept for the whole climb. From the per-land-run option it keeps a cost for dying: the
  land's enemies come back and (Claude's proposal) the unspent embers (now Shards) being carried are lost.
- **Proposal**: what the single run was for moves elsewhere. Commitment comes from take-or-consume, which is
  permanent for the climb; a varying order comes from the ride after each god offering two or three lands within
  the tier; replay comes from the "everything undone" ending, each climb started with unlocked weapons and elements
  and harder than the last. The hub is reached from any rest fire, as the place the sponsor speaks.
- **Liked (user)**: each land is about three districts, one elite and the god, with side paths that hold the lore
  and the land's plain weapon.
- **Leaning (user, 2026-11-17)**: the first land takes a brand-new player about 30 to 40 minutes and an experienced
  player about 20. Claude's reading: that is the time of a clean pass, the walk and the fights won, with deaths and
  retries on top; side paths are extra and optional.
- **Decided (user, 2026-11-17)**: later lands grow a little. The two duo lands are one and a half times to almost
  twice the size, because they hold two gods. The final area is a bit shorter, since the weight there is on the last
  fight.
- **Proposal** (clean pass, new player): Fire 30 to 40 minutes; the other early solo lands about 40; the middle solo
  lands about 45; each duo land 55 to 70; the seat 15 to 20 before the last champion; no walk before the sponsor.
  About six hours for a first climb before deaths; perhaps three and a half for an experienced player.
- **Proposal**: the sponsor tells one story aloud; the carvings and remains in each land quietly tell another, about
  the god murdered there before the usurper came.
- This replaces the run-based decisions in `docs/GAME.md` ("run-based, and every run includes all the bosses", the
  hub waking after every fall) once the user confirms it.

### Level design rules for every land (decided 2026-11-18, evening)

Fire's land is planned room by room in `docs/design/fire_levels.md`; these rules carry that work to the other lands.
- **Decided (user)**: each land's map takes its shape from the land's rule, not from Fire's straight march.
  - Water: islands round a lagoon, joined by causeways that open at low tide.
  - Metal: a ring road along the giant's round, with the labyrinth in the middle.
  - Ice: a funnel climbing from the harbour through the forest to the giant's bones.
  - Poison: a city with a ground deck and a rooftop deck for the miasma hours.
  - Lightning and Wind: the two criss-crossing stairs.
  - Earth and Life: the fork already decided.
- **Decided (user)**: rest fires follow Fire's rhythm.
  - About one every 6 to 8 minutes of a new player's walk, and always one at the god's door.
  - Every district opens one shortcut loop back to its fire.
  - Roughly 5 to 7 fires per land, more in the duo lands.
- **Decided (user)**: two kinds of optional discovery in every land.
  - **See it, then find the way**: a reward or place shown from the main path but out of reach, and reached later by
    a route the player finds. This is the main kind, and it holds the land's plain weapon and lore.
  - **One optional trial arena**: a harder mix of the land's enemies, or a lone mini-elite, guarding a Memory or a
    Body piece.
  - Hidden walls and side-quest people were not chosen as standard parts of a land.
- **Decided (user)**: each district has up to three walkable levels.
  - The levels are joined by ramps, stairs and ladders that can be seen from above.
  - Nothing walkable lies under a roof; roofs and overhangs only fade. This is the lesson of Sun City's playtest.
- **Decided (user)**: the four lands with a clock each get their own verb, so none of them is "wait for the cycle".
  - **Fire, dash for shade**: the heat is places, not times. Sun-struck zones are crossed from shade to shade. This
    replaces the "times of high heat" leaning.
  - **Poison, climb**: the one true clock. The gongs ring the hours and the player climbs above the miasma.
  - **Metal, watch**: the giant is always in sight, so the player times a crossing by where it is.
  - **Water, turn**: the player turns the tide at tide stones, reshaping the map as a puzzle.
- **Decided (user)**: the three lands where something sweeps open ground each get their own verb.
  - **Ice, stealth and sight lines**: the player breaks the huntress's line of sight, and can strike back; a hit
    makes her fall back.
  - **Lightning and Wind, rhythm**: the rivals' race passes on a drumbeat heard coming. A well-timed dodge through it
    is rewarded, not only sheltering under a gate.
  - **Earth and Life, route-finding**: the Wild Hunt is one set piece of choosing which standing stones to run
    between.
- **Decided (user)**: Metal's labyrinth shifts on the giant's footsteps.
  - Each time its round passes overhead, the tread shakes the maze into its next layout.
  - There are a few fixed layouts, all shown on a bronze map plaque in each hub room, so the cycle can be learned.
- **Decided (user)**: the huntress's arrows on the walk are heavy but always warned.
  - About a second ahead, a glint on a ridge and a thin line on the ground show where the arrow will land.
  - A hit deals heavy damage and pins the player for a moment, so the pack catches up.
- **Decided (user)**: Water's tide stones flip the whole lagoon between two states, low and high.
  - Low opens the reef paths and wrecks, and the bait does best in the shallows.
  - High raises the drowned, floats the player over walls and up to high ledges.
  - Stones stand only at certain places, so a chosen tide holds until the next stone.
- **Decided (user)**: in the Poison land the player climbs at marked points.
  - Fixed ladders, stairs, carts and drainpipes, each marked by a red lantern so it reads from above.
  - There are enough of them that one is always 15 to 20 seconds away when the first gong warns.
  - No new climbing system is needed.
- **Decided (user)**: in Earth and Life's land, the barrows age the wild in both directions.
  - Going through one way ages Life's side a year; going back the other way makes it a year younger.
  - No path is ever lost for good.
- **Decided (user)**: the hub and the gods' council are two different places.
  - The hub is the sponsor's own place.
  - The council belongs only to the last land, so its first sight stays fresh.
- **Decided (user)**: the hub is a shore where every ride out ends.
  - Each ride stays moored there as a trophy: the canoe, the chariot, the longship, the crane on its post, the storm
    cloud overhead, the sunset that never finishes.
  - The sea there never moves and the waves stand still mid-break, a quiet clue that the sponsor is Time.
- **Leaning (user)**: the hub is where the sponsor collects the elements, so that it can consume them properly, along
  with the player's body. **Proposal** for joining this to the shore:
  - What each ride brings in is the element the player carries, laid up in the hub, and not only a trophy.
  - The hub fills as a store, not as a home: each element taken gets its vessel, or its place in a growing pattern
    on the shore.
  - At the centre is a place made for one body, the vessel being prepared. Early on it reads as a shrine for the
    player; late, it reads as an altar.
- **Decided (user)**: it reads as a shrine at first and turns into an altar.
  - Each element brought is set in its place, glowing, as if in the player's honour.
  - As the climb goes on, small things turn: the pattern plainly needs a body at its centre, the vessels lean inward,
    and the sponsor's words about the player shift from "champion" to "vessel".
  - Careful players see it coming; everyone else gets the turn at the end.
- **Decided (user)**: the Water god's voyaging canoe is his own, waiting on the beach where the fight began. He lets
  the player take it as his last act. This also avoids repeating the Ice land's longship rising.
- **Decided (user)**: each land's optional trial is different, invented for that land (a duel in one, a survival
  wave in another, a race in the Lightning land, and so on).
- **Decided (user)**: in Earth and Life's land the gods' brawl is felt everywhere and seen only at the seats.
  - Everywhere: distant thunder of stone, the roar of the wild, birds lifting in flocks, and now and then a tremor.
  - The fight itself is seen only from the two seats and on the last walk.
- **Decided (user, 2026-11-19)**: land-wide rules from the cross-land kit list (`docs/design/kit_list.md` section 5).
  - **Held shots**: one per district; rides out are not counted.
  - **Roofs fade everywhere.** Shadow-only is kept only where the user has allowed it (Poison's plank bridges). Fire's
    hall, colonnades and bridge-house are to be checked against this when Fire is next revised.
  - **Warned arrows are one piece with rules per source**: the huntress's are red, perfect guard only, with a 0.10 s
    window; the last land's arrows on the walk and Metal's bronze feathers are pale, with the standard 0.18 s window.
  - **One sight test for watchers above the player**: a line test against cover with a lee check at build time, for
    the huntress and for the giant; the giant's light stays real and is made to agree with it.
  - **One depth rule for water**, the Water land's: under 0.3 m is as on land, 0.3 to 1.0 m is wading. Lightning and
    Wind's wash and Poison's mercury stay their own surfaces; the throw-back costs 10 in water and 12 in mercury.
  - **One drop piece** of 1.4 to 4 m, with each land's own landing rule checked in the editor from the recorded
    landings.
  - Also settled there: one lightning strike piece with one look, its shape per use; the last land's frozen Hunt and
    storm people use Earth and Life's and Lightning and Wind's own rules.

#### The land worked out (2026-11-18, with the user)

- **Decided (user)**: the two gods are **rivals, forever fighting over who is faster** (Earth and Life are allies who
  forgot; these two never were).
- **Decided (user)**, the story fold, which reaches every land: each first god was killed by Time in its own land;
  Light and Dark saw what was happening, went to the gods' gathering place, and their deaths there broke the sponsor.
  So each land's murdered god is that element's first god. Here two first gods died: the original thunder and wind,
  nods to Raijin and Fujin. **Proposal**: the summit's painted screens show them as one storm, facing each other
  across a gold field like the famous folding screens; Narukami and the Wind god, who sit in their seats, are
  corrupted copies who can only race each other.
- **Decided (user)**, the shape: the climb starts at a **shore of old ruins**, goes up **two stairs of torii gates
  that criss-cross up the mountain** (the rivals' racecourse: **Proposal** scorched gates on one, wind-split gates on
  the other, swapping the lead at every crossing), to a **split summit**: one great hall in two mirrored halves,
  Narukami's castle on one side and the Wind god's monastery on the other, equal, so neither seems the lesser. Then
  through the clouds to fight in the heavens; when the beasts come, the summit's top breaks away and the player walks
  on clouds.
- **Decided (user)**: the shore is the **drowned shrine of the two murdered first gods**: a great torii standing in
  the surf (a nod to Itsukushima) is the first gate of the stair, with a wind-scoured shrine town falling into the
  sea. Not the mortal army of the opening.
- **Decided (user)**: the Wind god's beast is the **white tiger**; **the dragon and the tiger** are the land's emblem
  ("clouds follow the dragon, wind follows the tiger"): carved on the gates, painted on the screens, and the two
  beasts of the last phase.
- **Decided (user)**: the fight is **a race to the player**: both fight on the ground, and every attack is a race to
  reach the player first.
- **Decided (user)**, the race rules: every attack shows both racers' paths converging on the player (Narukami's a
  jagged cyan line, the Wind god's a pale streak); the shorter path shows who arrives first. Only the winner's blow
  lands and the loser's is cut short, so the player never takes two blows from one race. When their paths cross, the
  slower crashes into the faster and both stagger, so the player can bait them into each other.
- **Decided (user)**: the transformation is a race too. When one is beaten down it becomes its beast to be faster,

### Poison's land: the court alchemist (Chinese)

- **Decided (user)**:

  - Hook swords that link into one long weapon.
  - Poison needles that stick in the floor and form grids the player must cross.

  - Centipede: coils round the jar wall and tightens into a shrinking ring; burrows and erupts beneath the player;

  - Snake: lunges from a coil, spits venom, constricts a caught player; sheds its skin, leaving a decoy that strikes

  - Scorpion: the armoured tank; pincer grabs; a tail sting that leaves poison pools and, dodged at the right moment,

    poisons it for a while.
  - Eating the dead: a dead creature's corpse lies for a few seconds; if another reaches it, it eats it, grows and

  all the combinations. Playtest the nastiest pairings.
- **Proposal**, reconciling denial with the fusion: a destroyed corpse leaves a dark residue; when the last creature
  falls, all the remains, eaten or shattered, crawl back together into the man.
- **Proposal**: only two creatures are on the floor; the others circle the rim of the jar and throw only slow, clearly
  marked attacks; when one falls another drops in. At each gong, poison gas fills the bottom of the jar and the
  player climbs onto shelves in the wall or onto the scorpion's back.
