# The world, its gods and the story behind them

Notes from the think session between the user and Claude on 2026-11-16 and 17. Each item carries a status, as in
`docs/GAME.md`: **Decided** (the user said so), **Leaning** (the user's current preference), **Proposal** (Claude's
idea, not yet accepted), **Open**. Where this file and `docs/GAME.md` differ, this file is newer; the two are to be
brought together once the user has confirmed the open points.

## How the world is laid out

- **Leaning (user)**: each area is its own themed land, drawn from one region's mythology. After a god falls the
  player boards a themed means of travel and the game cuts forward to the next land. The ride belongs to the land
  being left, so it still works when the order of gods changes inside a tier.
- **Decided (user)**: every godly second form is a mythological or godly creature.
- **Decided (user)**: each god's name is an original name with the sound and feel of its source, a nod and never the
  deity's own name.
- **Decided (user)**: some duo fights stay, reworked into pairs that make sense in myth.

- **Decided (user, 2026-11-17)**: the first land is Fire's, the sun-king's city. The player starts there with fists
  and no element, and takes the first element from the Fire god. This replaces the earlier decision in
  `docs/GAME.md` that the first run starts with fists and one chosen element.
- **Proposal**: because the player has nothing to grow yet, the first god is simply taken from; the take-or-consume
  choice first appears at the second god.
- **Decided (user, 2026-11-17)**: after the demo, the ordinary enemies and the area of the Lightning land are fully
  reworked to fit its theme much better.
- **Liked as first drafts (user, 2026-11-17)**: the sketches of each land's feel, walk, god's room and ride that
  Claude gave in the session (sun-king's city, fished-up islands, huntress's fjord, smith's foundry, alchemist's
  court, the stones and the grove, the seat under the eclipse, and the last fight in the hub). To be written out
  here land by land as each is worked through.

### Fire's land: the sun-king's city

- **Liked (user, 2026-11-17)**: four kinds of corruption by fire, of the six Claude offered. *The wick*: they burn
  and are not consumed; the wrapped dead smoulder like candle wicks and the living are charred with ember light in
  the cracks; they grab and hold to share the burning. *The gilded*: the sun-king promised his people the golden flesh
  of gods and it came true as molten gold, masks fused to faces; the more gold, the higher the rank. *Sun-blind*:
  they looked at their god until their eyes burned out, and hunt by heat and by shadow. *The thirst*: fire as
  drought; dried husks that crave anything wet and may go for the player's flasks.
- **Proposal**: the wick and the gilded are the land's ladder of rank (common people, then soldiers and priests,
  then the god as the sun); the sun-blind and the thirsting are single enemy types inside it. It is always noon there.
- **Leaning (user, 2026-11-17)**: lands need larger first areas to explore and to carry the land's lore, even if
  sparse in the manner of Elden Ring.
- **Proposal (Claude, 2026-11-17), the land in three districts, an elite and the god**:
  1. *The dry river*: where the player wakes. A dead riverbed, cracked mud, reed boats lying where the water left
     them, a harbour with no water. The thirsting. Fists only; the first rest fire. Landmark: a great barque stranded
     on the mud, which is the ride out at the end. Side path: a sealed well shrine.
  2. *The city of wicks*: mud-brick streets and the embalmers' quarter. The wicks. Narrow alleys, grabbing hands;
     the plain spear is found at a gatehouse. Side path: painted tombs that show the star falling in the desert and a
     mortal carrying it home, and under the paint an older god of the hearth and the dawn.
  3. *The golden way*: the avenue of seated statues, their faces recut from the old god's to the king's, and the
     hall of columns before the court. The gilded, and the sun-blind in the columns' light and shade.
  - *Elite*: one of the wholly gilded, a champion cast in gold who holds the last gate with a spear and shows the
    god's patterns before the god does.
  - *The god's room*: an open sun-court with an obelisk whose shadow turns like a clock hand. First a spear duel
    with the sun-king as a man. Then the form: the scarab that rolls the sun across the court and the falcon that
    carries it up, one creature in two shapes, as the sun was a beetle at dawn and a falcon at noon. The sun is the
    shared piece of the fight. When the whole court burns, the obelisk's moving shadow is the only safe ground.
  - The player has no choice here but to take Fire. Then the barque.

## How a climb is structured

- **Leaning (user, 2026-11-17)**: the single run in the manner of Hades gives way to this, which the user called
  "B with C" of the options offered. Death returns the player to the last rest fire in the land they are in, in the
  manner of Elden Ring and Sekiro: the sponsor turns time back a little, not all the way. Gods already beaten stay
  beaten and the build is kept for the whole climb. From the per-land-run option it keeps a cost for dying: the
  land's enemies come back and (Claude's proposal) the unspent embers being carried are lost.
- **Proposal**: what the single run was for moves elsewhere. Commitment comes from take-or-consume, which is
  permanent for the climb; a varying order comes from the ride after each god offering two or three lands within
  the tier; replay comes from the "everything undone" ending, each climb started with unlocked weapons and elements
  and harder than the last. The hub is reached from any rest fire, as the place the sponsor speaks.
- **Liked (user)**: each land is about three districts, one elite and the god, with side paths that hold the lore
  and the land's plain weapon.
- **Leaning (user, 2026-11-17)**: the first land takes a brand-new player about 30 to 40 minutes and an experienced
  player about 20. Claude's reading: that is the time of a clean pass, the walk and the fights won, with deaths and
  retries on top; side paths are extra and optional.
- **Proposal**: the sponsor tells one story aloud; the carvings and remains in each land quietly tell another, about
  the god murdered there before the usurper came.
- This replaces the run-based decisions in `docs/GAME.md` ("run-based, and every run includes all the bosses", the
  hub waking after every fall) once the user confirms it.

### The Water god's fight

- **Decided (user)**: a duel on the beach first. In the second phase the form rises and drags the islets under while
  the player fights on shrinking ground; then the player is pulled under. The fight may be longer than the others to
  make room for the moving set pieces.
- **Proposal**: "under" is the eye of a whirlpool: the bare seabed with walls of spinning water round it, wrecks on the
  floor and arms reaching in through the water, so the player moves and dodges as everywhere else. An arm can be cut
  while it holds an islet. The fall into the whirlpool is the breather between the two halves.

## The story behind it

- **Decided (user)**: the sponsor is the one who did it. It was greedy, killed its fellow gods to gather their power,
  and in doing so the elements were split and scattered. The true last fight of the game is against the sponsor, who
  fights with its power over time.
- **Proposal, built on that**: there were twelve first gods, one for each element and one for time, forces of the
  whole world and of no one land. Time ruled nothing and only watched; it saw that all of them would end, and chose
  to be the one that did not. It killed the others one by one and took each element into itself.
- **Decided (user)**: the breaking was the dying gods' last act, and Light and Dark's in particular.
- **Decided (user)**: Light and Dark were Time's siblings.
- **Proposal**: they were the last it came for. Dying, they broke it open: the elements it had swallowed were thrown
  across the world beyond its reach, and it was left with only time and no body, which is why the sponsor is a voice
  and an outcast. Light and Dark fell together, which is why one bearer holds both.
- **Proposal**: in each land the first mortal to seize a fallen element became that land's god, and its myths grew
  round them. The elements were torn from murdered gods and are not at peace: they corrupt whoever holds them, and the
  corruption spreads to those who follow them.
- **Proposal**: the elements will not answer their murderer, and a mortal has room for one. So the sponsor empties a
  mortal: it takes the player's past, which leaves room for all of them. What it tells the player is true as far as
  it goes; it leaves out who killed the gods the usurpers replaced.
- **Decided (user)**: the bearer of Light and Dark is the champion before the player. They went after Light and Dark
  first and learned the truth from them. The knowledge drove them partly mad, so they can only tell the player once
  they are beaten.
- **Leaning (user)**: at the end, with the sponsor beaten and its power over time in hand, the player can turn every
  element back to its pure form so that it no longer corrupts. The user is not sure this is the best ending.
- **Leaning (user, 2026-11-17)**: the player chooses between two endings once the sponsor is beaten.
  *The watcher*: the player takes Time into themselves to turn the elements back to their pure form; the world is
  healed and the player is the twelfth god, who rules nothing and watches. *Everything undone*: time is turned back
  so far that none of it has happened, and only the player remembers; the user sees this as the answer to why the
  game can be played again.
- **Decided (user, 2026-11-17)**: the second playthrough is harder. How it gets harder is left as ideas for now:
  a changed order of gods, harder fights or an added move, words that change because the player knows what the
  sponsor is, the last champion recognising the player, a way out that the first run cannot reach.
- **Proposal**: the choice is set out as every choice after a god was, two things left on the floor. The watcher is
  the ending that closes the story.
- **Open**: the sponsor's name and form; what the player's past was; what carries over into a replay.

### The fight against the sponsor (proposals)

It mends its own wounds by turning them back unless the player breaks the marks it sets; it plays the player's
earlier deaths back as echoes of themselves; it slows the player or runs its own attacks forward; it ages the stage
to ruin and back; and it will not let the player die, because it needs the vessel, so the fight is won only by doing
the one thing it cannot undo.
