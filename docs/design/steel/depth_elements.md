Written 2026-11-18 to `docs/design/steel/BRIEF.md`, answering the owner's request to cut the marks that do nothing
and still give the trees enough depth for a long game, with marks that "break the rules a little". Everything here
is **Proposal**. "Heresy" and "Evolution" are working names. Every number is a first guess for play to tune. The
weapon side of the same two layers is in `depth_weapons.md` (another writer); the two must share the slots below.

## Heresies: the shared rules

**What a Heresy is.** A rare mark that breaks one standard rule of the whole game (guard, dodge, health, mana, death,
moving) and puts something else in its place, with a cost that is felt in every fight. A keystone changes how one
element plays; a Heresy changes how the game plays. The story: the gods' power was never meant for a mortal body, and
each god's corruption began with a rule it broke. Taking their power, the player takes their heresy too.

| Rule | Proposal |
|---|---|
| **The slot** | One Heresy slot in the Hand, set at rest fires like everything else. It opens at the **third god** (the end of tier 2, about two hours in). A **second slot** opens at the **seventh god** (the end of the first duo land). The patron opens it: "Their laws were never yours." |
| **Shared with steel** | The weapon side's Heresies (`depth_weapons.md`; the Teacher leaves one) should sit in the same slots, so the player chooses whether their one broken rule is steel or element. Steel and element stay equal; reconcile with that file. |
| **What may be worn** | A Heresy works only while its element is carried. All in on one element, both of its Heresies may be worn. Two Heresies that break the same rule (the "Rule" column below) cannot be worn together; the Hand says which. |
| **The god's Heresy** | One per element, never sold. It lies where the god fell, under the mark of the older god the usurper murdered (the quiet second story of each land), and is picked up after the fall whether the god was taken or consumed. It waits, sealed, until the slot opens and the element is owned, as a gated technique waits. |
| **The deep Heresy** | One per element, **8 Shards**, growing from the element's Devotion or from either of its keystones. So it is the far end of the tree, never the near. |
| **Consume** | Consume (which strengthens an owned element) may give that element's deep Heresy free, as one of its offers. |
| **Seen** | Each Heresy puts one constant sign on the player's body, so the broken rule is readable from above at all times. |

Pairs worth chasing with two slots: Given Fire with Kneel to the Sun (cast without pause, pass the fire on with the

## What the research taught

- **Slay the Spire's boss relics**: each gives the core resource (one energy) and takes a whole axis away (no rest,
  no gold, no sight of intents). The cost that scales with skill (Runic Dome, Coffee Dripper) is the best kind: it
  is felt by a learner and played round by a master. Taken: Given Fire, Drawn From the Cloud, The Tide Comes Back.
- **Path of Exile's keystones**: they convert one system into another rather than add (life becomes 1 and chaos
  cannot hurt; mana becomes life; never miss, never crit). Taken: Count the Stones, Kneel to the Sun.
- **Balatro's rule jokers**: "every card counts as a face card", "two suits count as one", "every played card scores".
  Redefining a category makes the item combine with everything else. Taken: Burn counts as balance; a drum counts as
  a perfect guard; Static on the floor counts as mana.
- **Risk of Rain 2's lunar items**: a strong effect with a visible, permanent drawback (Shaped Glass, Transcendence),
  and Gesture of the Drowned, which halves cooldowns but fires the equipment by itself. Taken: They Move by
  Themselves, and the rule that each Heresy shows on the body.
- **Hades**: Chaos boons are a curse for a few rooms, then a blessing; Daedalus hammers rewrite a weapon's attack.
  Hades's duo boons need a boon from each of two gods. Taken: The Debt Waits (harm deferred); duo recipes.
- **Noita's perks**: Glass Cannon is useless alone and strong with Saving Grace; perks that combine are the joy.
  Taken: the second slot, and the pairs above.
- **Dark Souls**: the Ring of Favor and Protection breaks forever if removed; a vow, not an item. Not taken: here
  nothing permanent can go wrong (`STEEL_AND_ELEMENT.md` 5.5); the cost lives only while it is worn.
- **Elden Ring's Rivers of Blood** and its kin: one build-defining item that a whole build grows round. A Heresy
  should be the headline of a build, the thing a player names when asked what they run.
- **Vampire Survivors' evolutions**: a weapon at its top level, a particular passive item, and a chest after ten
  minutes; the recipes are not shown until found. Taken whole as a principle for Evolutions.

## Fire

### Given Fire (Khepharu's Heresy)

- **Breaks**: spells wait on cooldowns.
- **Instead**: basic and duo spells have no cooldown. Each cast sets you burning one deeper, five at most; at five you
  cannot cast. Each weapon blow that lands takes one Burn off you and lays 2 Burn on what it struck. A perfect guard
  puts one out.
- **Cost**: your Burn eats 1 health a second a stack, and an unguarded blow taken adds one more. Mana spells and the
  surge still cost mana. Masters that reset a cooldown take a Burn off you instead.
- **Why it is Fire**: "I promised them gold. I gave them fire." The sun-king's fire is carried in the body and must be
  given away: cast, cast, cast, then cut the fire out of yourself into them. It is the steel-and-element weave as a
  rule.
- **From above**: rings of real flame climb the player, one a stack, feet to crown, with smoke off the top. Each blow
  throws a tongue of flame down the blade into the enemy, and a ring goes out.
- **Earned**: in the hearth room under the sun-court, the older god of the hearth and the dawn. When Khepharu falls
  its cold hearth lights by itself. The fire in it is the Heresy.

### Kneel to the Sun

- **Breaks**: an enemy kneels when blows fill its stagger meter.
- **Instead**: Burn is balance. An enemy reaching five Burn kneels at once, open to the finisher, and its Burn is
  spent. An elite must reach five twice within 8 s; a god takes a third of its meter for each five.
- **Cost**: nothing else fills a stagger meter: blows, breakers and perfect guards break no balance. Breaker spells lay
  2 Burn in place of their stagger. What will not burn will not kneel.
- **Why it is Fire**: Sekhmet's fire brought men down before the sword reached them. A Hand of openers becomes a Hand
  of breakers; Fire Without End (burns deepen alone) becomes a slow tide of kneeling.
- **From above**: the Burn pips under an enemy are drawn as a ring that fills. At five the flame on it stands up in a
  column and it drops to one knee inside the fire.
- **Earned**: the deep Heresy, 8 Shards, from Fire Without End or either Fire keystone.

## Lightning

### The Drums Answer (Narukami's Heresy)

- **Breaks**: a perfect guard is a press timed to the blow.
- **Instead**: your blows beat drums. Every fourth weapon blow that lands sets a drum behind you, three at most. The
  first blow that would strike you while a drum stands, from any side and red ones too, is struck down by a bolt
  before it lands. It counts as a perfect guard (mana, cooldown cuts, Masters, In One Breath) and spends the drum.
- **Cost**: your own guard has no perfect window; a timed press is only a block. Drums come from blows only, never
  from spells. With none standing you are on your guard and dodge alone.
- **Why it is Lightning**: "I only wanted the storm to answer me." Now it does: the storm guards for you while you
  keep cutting, and the skill moves from timing the guard to keeping the drums full.
- **From above**: three round drum faces of black cloud on the player's back, as Raijin's ring, crackle round their
  rims. A beat throws a flat ring of dust off the floor, a bolt falls on the attacker, and the drum goes dark.
- **Earned**: at the summit, in the ring of drums Narukami beat. When he falls one drum is left whole.

### Drawn From the Cloud

- **Breaks**: mana is earned by your blows and held in you.
- **Instead**: you have no mana bar. Mana is the Static on your enemies. A mana spell, the surge, the deep strike or
  the weapon skill draws Static off every enemy within 10 m, 8 mana a stack (a god's stacks count three times), and
  each stack drawn strikes its bearer for 4 as it leaves.
- **Cost**: no mana carried between fights or earned by blows. A room with no Static on it gives nothing. Static drawn
  this way does not discharge.
- **Why it is Lightning**: the dry storm. The power is in the cloud you have built over them, and you pull it down.
- **From above**: crackling threads run from every charged enemy into the player's raised hand as a spell is cast,
  and the Static on them goes dark. The HUD's mana bar becomes a count of the Static on the floor.
- **Earned**: the deep Heresy, from In One Breath or either Lightning keystone.

## Wind

### What the Bag Holds (the Wind god's Heresy)

- **Breaks**: you choose which of your four spells to cast.
- **Instead**: the four slots are one bag. Every spell you have learned of the carried elements, and their duo
  spells, turns in it; any of the four buttons casts the one at the bag's neck, and the next two are shown. A spell
  from the bag is 40% stronger, and all of them share one cooldown of 3 s.
- **Cost**: you do not choose what comes. The bag is a deck: every spell once before any twice, shuffled when it
  empties. Shapes are still set at the rest fire.
- **Why it is Wind**: the wind god opens the bag and whatever wind is in it comes out. You fight the storm you are
  dealt, and read the next two like a hand of cards.
- **From above**: the hide bag at the hip swells, and dust leaks from its neck in the shape of the next spell's role
  icon. The centre of the HUD's diamond shows the next two.
- **Earned**: in the Lightning and Wind land, the god's empty bag where he falls. It opens once more.

### The Wind Is Not Struck

- **Breaks**: to avoid a blow you must dodge it.
- **Instead**: while you move at full speed, the first blow that would strike you in each 2 s bends you aside 1.5 m in
  a streak of dust: no harm, and it counts as a perfect dodge. The dodge button becomes a hop of 3 m for 10 stamina,
  with no moment of safety.
- **Cost**: standing still, guarding, charging a heavy, casting a spell with a wind-up or drinking, you take 30% more.
  Red attacks bend you only from the front. Rings, floods and other blows over an area are never bent.
- **Why it is Wind**: what is never still is never struck. The fight is spent in motion, and every stop is a choice.
- **From above**: while moving, a pennant of dust and dry grass streams off the player's shoulders; it drops when
  they stop, so the open moment shows. A bend is a sharp sideways smear of dust.
- **Earned**: the deep Heresy, from On the Wind or either Wind keystone.

## Ice

### The Endless Slope (the huntress's Heresy)

- **Breaks**: you walk where you steer.
- **Instead**: you ski. Speed builds over 1 s to half again a run; turns are arcs; let go of the stick and you glide to
  a stop over 2 m. A ski line of ice stays behind you 4 s (1 Chill a second on enemies crossing it). Blows landed at
  full speed strike 30% harder and Chill. The dodge is a carve that keeps your speed and throws snow (1 Chill in 2 m).
- **Cost**: you cannot stop dead or turn on the spot. Guarding brakes you to a halt over 0.4 s. No heavy blow can be
  charged above half speed. Narrow rooms are hard.
- **Why it is Ice**: "The hunt never ended." She never stopped moving: strike, glide past, circle, come again.
- **From above**: two clean ski lines on any floor, snow thrown at each carve, breath fog streaming back.
- **Earned**: the huntress's skis, left standing in the snow by the broken lake where she falls.

### The Hour Freezes

- **Breaks**: a perfect dodge slows time for a third of a second.
- **Instead**: a perfect dodge freezes everything within 10 m for 1.5 s: enemies, blows in the air, arrows, hazards.
  You move and strike freely. Your blows are held in the frozen and land together when time runs again; an enemy that
  took three or more is Frozen outright. Elites freeze 0.8 s; gods are only slowed to half for 1 s.
- **Cost**: once every 10 s. Perfect dodges give no mana and cut no cooldowns. A plain dodge costs twice the stamina.
- **Why it is Ice**: the long winter that stops the world while the hunter takes the shot.
- **From above**: rime races out over the floor in a ring from the player and up every body, blow and arrow it
  reaches, so the stop reads as winter, not a clock; the world goes grey under it. On release every frozen enemy
  cracks white at once.
- **Earned**: the deep Heresy, from Deep Winter or either Ice keystone.

## Water

### The Tide Comes Back (the fisher's Heresy)

- **Breaks**: health lost stays lost until something heals it.
- **Instead**: every wound ebbs and comes back. The damage of a blow goes out with the tide and comes back in over 6 s,
  all of it, unless another blow strikes you first; then the first tide is lost.
- **Cost**: you carry no flasks; the flask button and Water's flask marks do nothing. Struck twice inside 6 s, both
  blows stand. A god's combo of three is three blows.
- **Why it is Water**: "I pulled up islands... and drowned them." The sea gives back what it takes, if you are not
  caught twice. A fighter who takes one blow, then dances for six seconds.
- **From above**: a ring of water round the player's feet drains outward to a tide line when struck and runs back in;
  a tide broken by a second blow crashes white and sinks into the floor.
- **Earned**: on the bared reef where the octopus falls, the one tide pool that does not drain.

### Water Takes the Blow

- **Breaks**: the guard covers the front, costs stamina, and lets a third through.
- **Instead**: the guard covers every side, costs no stamina and never breaks. It stops nothing: every blow guarded
  comes through whole as grey health, which does not fade while you guard. Each weapon blow you land wins back grey
  health twice its damage.
- **Cost**: grey health is lost to an unguarded blow as now, and red attacks still go through, so one red blow takes
  the whole store and its own damage. Perfect guards work as ever.
- **Why it is Water**: the reef that takes the swell. The sea breaks on it whole, and the lagoon behind lies still
  until the tide gives it back. A guard that invites the blow and must be paid off with the blade.
- **From above**: guarded blows splash into a sheet of water that hangs round the body and swells with the store;
  each blow landed pours some of it back into the player.
- **Earned**: the deep Heresy, from The Sea Comes In or either Water keystone.

## Life

### Grown Through Them (Carnwyn's Heresy)

- **Breaks**: an affliction fades.
- **Instead**: Seeded never fades, and grows a depth every 4 s. At full depth the enemy takes root where it stands for
  good: it can turn and strike but never move again. Elites root 4 s at a time; gods are slowed 30% instead.
- **Cost**: the seed no longer mends you (the root's mending is off). A rooted enemy cannot be
  thrown, pulled or lifted.
- **Why it is Life**: "I let the forest grow through them." Sow a room, step back, and watch it become a wood.
- **From above**: bark climbs the enemy from the feet, a band a depth; at full it is a trunk to the waist with roots in
  the floor and buds at the shoulders.
- **Earned**: in the grove where Carnwyn falls, the oak splits, and the seed in its heart is the Heresy.

### The Club's Two Ends

- **Breaks**: blows harm and only healing mends.
- **Instead**: the weapon has two ends. Light blows strike with the killing end, half again as hard. Heavy blows strike
  with the living end: they do no damage, but mend you by what they would have done, with full stagger. Finishers
  strike with the killing end.
- **Cost**: nothing else heals you: no flasks, no mending marks, no surge's healing. A heavy blow never kills.
- **Why it is Life**: the Dagda's club killed with one end and raised the dead with the other. Every swing is a choice
  between taking life and taking it back.
- **From above**: the weapon is bound in black bark at one end and green leaf at the other. A heavy blow throws a green
  burst from the enemy into the player's chest; light blows leave black splinters.
- **Earned**: the deep Heresy, from Wood Grows Back or either Life keystone.

## Earth

### Count the Stones (Brannoch's Heresy)

- **Breaks**: health is a number that blows wear down.
- **Instead**: no health bar. You are five standing stones. Each attack that strikes you, large or small, breaks one
  (every hit of one swing counts as one). The fifth falls and you fall. A perfect guard or a finisher sets one back
  up, once each 6 s; each flask sets one back.
- **Cost**: a rat's nip costs as much as a god's greatest blow. Grey health and every heal by points are gone. An
  affliction or hazard breaks one stone for each 4 s you stand in it.
- **Why it is Earth**: "I raised the stones so they'd remember me." Stone does not wear; it breaks. Against a god's
  great blows you stay whole; against a swarm you must not be touched.
- **From above**: five grey standing stones ring the player's feet in a 1 m circle. A blow topples one with a crack and
  a puff of dust; set back up, it rises out of the floor.
- **Earned**: at the meeting ground, where Brannoch falls among his stones. One stone of his ring stands for you.

### The Circle Speaks

- **Breaks**: your spells come from you.
- **Instead**: every spell you cast also comes from each of your stones standing (stones, boulders, basalt columns,
  four at most), at half strength, aimed at the enemy nearest each stone.
- **Cost**: you can cast only within 5 m of a stone of yours. Stone Rise and Under the Mound are the exceptions, so
  there is always a way to raise one; away from your stones the other spell buttons are dark.
- **Why it is Earth**: the circle answers its maker. With Stone Remembers, the longer the fight, the louder the ring.
- **From above**: a line of ochre dust runs over the floor from the player to each stone as a spell goes, and the
  stone throws grit as it casts.
- **Earned**: the deep Heresy, from Stone Remembers or either Earth keystone.

## Metal

### Bronze Does Not Tire (the smith's Heresy)

- **Breaks**: stamina is spent and runs out.
- **Instead**: there is no stamina. Swings, guards, dodges and runs cost nothing, and no guard breaks for lack of it.
- **Cost**: the bronze cannot yield. The dodge has no perfect window (no perfect dodges, so none of their mana, slowed
  time or cooldown cuts), and a blow that lands on you from behind seizes you for 0.6 s.
- **Why it is Metal**: "I built a body that wouldn't limp. I never left it." Tireless and relentless, and open at its
  back.
- **From above**: bronze plates on the arms and shins with a hot seam between; they ring on each blow taken. Seized,
  the seams flash red and steam.
- **Earned**: in the foundry where the bronze giant falls, a greave of its bronze left on the anvil.

### They Move by Themselves

- **Breaks**: you cast your spells.
- **Instead**: your four spells cast themselves the moment they are ready, at the nearest enemy; a counter casts itself
  when an enemy within 4 m begins an attack. Every cooldown is halved.
- **Cost**: no aim and no timing, so Masters tied to timing never fire, and no spell can be held for the right moment.
  Movement spells never cast themselves (cast them by hand); mana spells are cast by hand.
- **Why it is Metal**: the smith's self-moving tripods and golden handmaidens doing the work while he works the hammer.
  The steel is in your hand; the magic goes by itself.
- **From above**: four small bronze tripods walk at the player's heels, one a spell. Each glows as its spell readies
  and clanks as it fires.
- **Earned**: the deep Heresy, from The Guard Holds or either Metal keystone.

## Light

### The Eye Above (the champion's Heresy, from the eagle)

- **Breaks**: a spell starts from you and reaches only so far.
- **Instead**: the camera stands a quarter further back, and every spell falls from the sky on Marked enemies anywhere
  in view: a spell for one enemy on the Marked one nearest the aim, a spell for an area on the middle of the Marked.
- **Cost**: spells do nothing to an enemy that is not Marked, and spells lay no Mark. Only your blows Mark (the root's
  chain-end and perfect guard, the charged blade, the infused strike): steel chooses, the sky strikes.
- **Why it is Light**: the eagle that sees the whole field; the archer who saw only the bird's eye. What you have
  marked cannot hide anywhere in sight.
- **From above**: a shaft of light made visible by dust comes down on each Marked enemy as a spell lands, with the
  spell's own substance (flame, frost, water) falling inside it.
- **Earned**: at the champion's fall, where the eagle came down.

### The Edge Kept Bright

- **Breaks**: the charged blade is lit by a spell and spent in three blows.
- **Instead**: the blade is lit by a critical blow and stays lit while your blows are critical; the first blow that is
  not puts it out. While lit, every blow throws the third charged blow's ray (5 m, Marks).
- **Cost**: spells no longer charge the blade. While it is out, your blows strike 15% weaker.
- **Why it is Light**: light that stays while you strike true. With Full Daylight (every blow on the Marked critical
  until the mark fades) it can stay lit a whole fight, and then go out on one careless swing.
- **From above**: gilt runs along the edge and every cut leaves a gold line hanging in the air; a plain blow flakes the
  gilt off grey.
- **Earned**: the deep Heresy, from Full Daylight or either Light keystone.

## Dark

### Death Passed On (the champion's Heresy, from the bull)

- **Breaks**: when your health runs out you fall (or Rise once).
- **Instead**: while a Cursed enemy stands within 12 m, a killing blow does not kill you. The death goes to the Cursed
  enemy nearest you: an ordinary enemy dies of it, an elite loses a third of its health, a god a tenth and kneels. You
  stand at 1 health. Once every 10 s.
- **Cost**: no Rise (the root's revive is off), and your health cannot rise above half while it is worn. With no Cursed
  near, death is death.
- **Why it is Dark**: Yama's noose draws out a soul the size of a thumb. Now you choose whose.
- **From above**: below a third of your health a thin black rope runs over the floor to the nearest Cursed enemy,
  redrawn as that changes. When it pays, the rope snaps tight and a small pale figure is drawn out of the enemy.
- **Earned**: at the champion's fall, where the bull lay down.

### The Debt Waits

- **Breaks**: a blow's harm lands when it strikes.
- **Instead**: blows that strike you are written down, not felt: no flinch, no harm yet. Each is a debt that comes due
  4 s later. Every weapon blow you land meanwhile pays a third of its damage off the oldest debt.
- **Cost**: debts cannot be guarded off, healed or drunk away, and what is still owed when due is paid at one and a
  quarter. You keep no grey health.
- **Why it is Dark**: Dark buys its fights on credit and pays them out of the enemy, as Yama's scribe keeps the ledger.
- **From above**: each debt is a ring of black ash marks round the player's feet that shrinks as it is paid. When one
  comes due, black smoke bursts out of the body.
- **Earned**: the deep Heresy, from Nothing Stays Buried or either Dark keystone.

## Poison

### The Last One in the Jar (the alchemist's Heresy)

- **Breaks**: you are as strong at a fight's end as at its start.
- **Instead**: each enemy you kill makes you stronger for the rest of the fight: 8% more damage from weapon and spell a
  kill, with no limit. The fight ends when no enemy has known of you for 8 s.
- **Cost**: each kill also lays a dose on you that does not fade until the fight ends and bites on the poison's beat.
  Nothing shields you from it, Tasted Every Herb included. In a duel it does nothing.
- **Why it is Poison**: "So this is what forever tastes like." The gu: the last creature alive in the jar has eaten all
  the others and become the poison. A long fight makes you the worst thing in the room, and the sickest.
- **From above**: a small creature crawls onto the player's shoulders with each kill (centipede, snake, scorpion,
  toad, spider, then again); the skin greens and drips.
- **Earned**: in the alchemist's court, the shards of the gu jar the god broke out of.

### All Turns to Venom

- **Breaks**: what enemies lay on you harms you.
- **Instead**: every affliction an enemy lays on you (Burn, Chill, Static, any) turns to venom in you and does none of
  its own harm. It is stored as doses, a stack a dose, ten at most, and each weapon blow pours two of them into what
  it strikes.
- **Cost**: stored venom bites you on the poison's beat, 1 a dose, until it is poured out (Tasted Every Herb does not
  stop it). Your own blows no longer poison: Venom's root is off, and only your spells and what enemies give you dose.
- **Why it is Poison**: Shennong and the realgar wine: poison against poison. Every curse they throw becomes your ink.
- **From above**: green veins climb the player's arms with each dose stored; each blow throws a string of venom off the
  blade, tinted at its root by what it was.
- **Earned**: the deep Heresy, from Tasted Every Herb or either Poison keystone.

## Evolutions: the shared rules

**What an Evolution is.** A Mastered spell (its Master mark bought) joined with one particular partner turns into a new,
stronger spell, as a weapon at its top level and a particular item become one in Vampire Survivors, or two gods'
boons become a duo boon in Hades.

| Rule | Proposal |
|---|---|
| **Partners** | A weapon aspect active on the Way; another element's Devotion bought, with that element carried; another element's spell in the same Hand; a reaction shape set on a duo branch (both elements carried); a Bond rank. |
| **Discovery** | The first time the recipe holds at a rest fire, a new mark grows from the spell's Master on the chart, cracked out of its diamond, with a line: "Uraeus has changed." Before that the chart shows nothing, and "Grows from" never names a partner. |
| **Price** | **5 Shards**. After that the slot offers the base spell or the evolved one, as a Shape is chosen. If the partner goes, the slot falls back to the base. |
| **Form** | An evolved spell has no Shapes. It keeps its base's role and payment (cooldown or mana), with 1 or 2 s more cooldown where it is much stronger, and keeps a stronger form of its Master. |
| **Hints** | Each recipe has a hint in the world: a carving, a line someone says, or an enemy using the evolved spell on the player first (as an elite shows its technique). The game never lists recipes; the codex records one once it is found. |
| **One reachable all in** | Every element has one Evolution whose partner is its own (a Bond rank or a weapon aspect), so an all-in player can find it. The other needs a second element. |

**The spare ring-mix spells.** All twelve are used: Lit Fuse, Plasma Lance, Storm Overhead, Whiteout, Creeping Ice,
Lotus Step, Thorn Wall, Trip Hammer, Sun Wheels, Black Sun, Grave Soil and Fire Eater. Each spare's base is one of
its two elements' spells and its partner is the other element, so the recipe echoes the mix it came from. Built as
mana spells, each now takes its base's payment and is reworked to be stronger than its built version.

**Partner kinds used**: weapon aspect 6, Bond rank 5, another element's spell 5, reaction shape 4, another element's
Devotion 2.

- **By Khepharu (20)**: Fire's root, three spells learned and empowered, one Shape.
- **By the second god (44)**: the fourth spell, two Shapes, the first Master, a major, Water's root and one spell.
- **By the third god (68)**: Water's second spell, the duo branch's Shape and duo spell, two more Masters, Fire's
  reaction lift. Given Fire goes in the new slot at no cost.
- **By the fifth god (124)**: Fire's Devotion and a keystone, Fire's deep Heresy, The Winged Sun, Water's Shapes and a
  major, a second duo branch begun.
- **By the end (230)**: Fire nearly whole (about 100 of 113), Water about half, three duo branches in part, the roots
  and a spell or two of the late elements, three or four Evolutions, two Heresies worn.

**Does any tree run dry?**
- **All in on one element, yes, around the fifth or sixth god.** A tree with its depth is about 113 Shards (34 marks,
  the deep Heresy, two Evolutions; the god's Heresy is given), 108 for a player who never mixes. A purist spending
  everything on it finishes by about 124. The duo lands then give four new elements in a row, which is the natural
  next step, and the second Heresy slot and Bond V arrive there without costing Shards. Recommended: once every tree
  the player carries is full, Shards may be laid in the rest fire and kept safe from death, so a purist is never
  punished for having nothing left to buy.
- **Too deep to see in one climb: Light, Dark and most duo branches.** Light and Dark are owned only at the
  champion's fall, so their trees (about 226 Shards) and their five recipes (Sun Wheels, Black Sun, The Chariot
  Crosses, Ridden, Grave Soil) are for the sponsor fight and the next climb. All the element content is about 2,260

- **Ice, Poison and the duo-land elements** sit in the middle: well seen by players who carry them, rarely all the way

5. Light and Dark's god's Heresies are given at the champion's fall, so the sponsor fight can be fought with them.
