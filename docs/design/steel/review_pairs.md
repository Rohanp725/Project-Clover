# Review of the 55 pairs: are the duos true combos? (2026-11-18)

**Applied 2026-11-18: the recommended fixes are now in the content files.** (What changed is listed in `README.md`,
"Review fixes applied (2026-11-18)". This page is kept as the record.)

A combat designer's read of every pair in `pairs_d1_d2.md`, `pairs_d3.md`, `pairs_d4.md` and `pairs_d5.md`, against
`BRIEF.md` ("both, not added"), `README.md`, `STEEL_AND_ELEMENT.md` section 6, the element files and the lands in
`WORLD.md` and `LOOKS.md`. It answers the owner's request: do the duo combos feel like a true combo of both, or both
empowering each other, and not a random move that does not tie in; and for the weak ones, what would hit harder.

Nothing in the content files is changed here. Everything below is **Proposal**. Numbers are first guesses in the
ranges the built spells use.

## The lead

| Verdict | Count | What it means here |
|---|---|---|
| **Strong** | **32** | A true combo of both substances, one idea from reaction to crown, it hits, it rewards steel. Tune, do not change. |
| **OK** | **16** | The core ties the two together, but one part (a shape, the crown, or the plain reaction) is quiet, borrowed or a repeat. Fix that part. |
| **Weak** | **7** | The core does not tie the two together, or repeats another pair. Rework the core. |

The writers did good work: most pairs are rooted in real chemistry, weather or the forge, and in a real meeting of
the two lands' myths. The weak ones fail in four ways: **one element does all the work** (Dark and Poison, Dark and
Ice), **two effects side by side** (Life and Dark, Light and Poison's lantern), **a repeat of another pair**
(Wind and Earth is Capsize, Earth and Fire is Earthlight and Turned to Stone, Poison and Water is Rot Roots), or
**the right image buried in a shape** while the plain reaction does something generic (Fish Poison, Hollow Trunk,
Logan Stone, the gu jar).

## The ten biggest fixes, ranked

1. **Wind and Earth: Topple is Capsize again.** Both knock an Unsteady enemy flat for 1.2 s. Make **Logan Stone** the
   reaction: it rocks under your next three blows, and the third throws it down with a quake. The knockdown is earned
   with steel, and Capsize keeps the instant fall.
2. **Poison and Water: no idea of its own.** The pool that passes bites is Rot Roots; the Silver Pool and The Hundred
   Rivers are quicksilver, the Metal and Poison substance. Make **Fish Poison** the reaction (a fouled slick where
   heavily dosed enemies float up stunned) and the crown **Poison the Lagoon**, a slick your blade stirs.
3. **Light and Poison: Burning Sap is Quicksilver at criticals.** Give it the hogweed's real delay: the sun wakes the
   sap, the poison holds back for 3 s while criticals raise weals, then it all lands doubled. The crown **Bared to the
   Sun** tears the shade off a place, in place of Bitter Lantern, which lays a mark and a dose side by side.
4. **Dark and Poison: Brood is a dose counter, and Dark does nothing.** Make the reaction **The Sealed Jar**, the gu:
   a black ring closes on the floor, the poisoned that die inside crawl into the one at its heart, and the jar breaks
   with everything. Kill order becomes the play. Re-aim Wasting Grip to seal the jar.
5. **Earth and Fire: a copy of two other pairs.** Baked Earth is Earthlight's star of glowing seams in orange; Fired
   Clay is Turned to Stone. Make it **Burnt Mound**: the shaken floor throws up three stones and the fire heats them
   red, and your weapon bats them across the room. Keep Vitrified Wall.
6. **Dark and Ice: Frostbite is a number, all Ice.** Make it **Stolen Warmth**: the curse takes its warmth and the cold
   is what is left. Each weapon blow draws warmth out of it into you and leaves a Chill behind.
7. **Life and Dark: Grave Roots is a hold plus lifesteal, and the fourth root hold.** Make it **Girdled**, the
   strangler fig: roots coil tighter with each blow, girdle it at four, and if it dies girdled it stands as a hollow
   trunk wall.
8. **The far-side cones in the Ice pairs.** Rime, Spall and Prism all throw a cone out of the enemy's far side. Rime
   keeps it. **Ice and Light** makes **Mock Suns** (the flank) its reaction; **Ice and Metal** swaps Spall for **Shed
   Plate** and moves Cold Short's payoff onto the armour so it stops repeating Thermal Shock.
9. **The arcs that every blow throws.** Metal and Lightning's Live Iron arcs to the nearest enemy on every blow, which
   is Lightning's own charged blade; Fire and Lightning's Flashover is two set-offs at half. Make Live Iron run **only
   from iron to iron**, re-aim **Flashover** on the real fact that flame conducts (the flame catches every discharge
   near it), and give Metal and Lightning the **Forged Bolt** shape in place of Draws the Bolt.
10. **Life and Light: Life-Herb is Red Harvest for free.** Every blow critical for 8 s is Life's own health-paid spell.
    Make the crown **Turned to the Sun**: a lit herb that every Seeded enemy turns to face, as plants turn to the light,
    so their backs are yours.

Next after these (in the options below): re-aim the built **Strangling Vine** at the flower (Water and Life), make
**Anvil Stone** the Forge's reaction (Earth and Metal), replace **The Weighted Net** with **The Sea Takes It** (Water and
Metal), and replace Poison and Wind's **Turned Back** with **Dust on the Blade**.

## Patterns across the 55

These are not one pair's fault; they are where several pairs reach for the same verb. Each fix above or below thins
one of them.

| Pattern | Where | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| A cone or line out of the enemy's far side | Rime, Spall, Prism, Fire Lance, Red Hot's sparks, Through the Throat, Down the Passage | Fine as a shared grammar of "where you stand aims it", but three are Ice. Keep Rime; move Prism and Spall (fix 8). |
| "It goes brittle, then the heavy blow cracks it" | Thermal Shock, Cold Short, Turned to Stone, Fired Clay, Red Ore, Split Rock | Keep Thermal Shock (damage) and Turned to Stone (stagger and kneel). Cold Short pays on the armour; Fired Clay goes. |
| A star of cracks round the enemy | Earthlight, Baked Earth, Blackdamp, Earthed, Taking Root | Earthlight keeps the gold star and Earthed the fern; Baked Earth becomes stones; Blackdamp should run (Chokedamp). |
| Roots that hold | Taking Root, Grave Roots, Late Frost, Held Down, Death Turned Back | Grave Roots becomes Girdled, which slows and tightens rather than holds. |
| A crown that only lays both afflictions in a zone | Bitter Lantern, The Hundred Rivers, Rime Over Rime, The Burning Lake, The Hag's Staff | The first two are replaced. The last three keep, because each has a weapon payoff; Rime Over Rime is the thinnest. |
| "N blows, then it bursts" | Paper Seal (5), Ice in the Blood (4), Cold Chisel (3), Kindling Point (3), Logan Stone (3) | Each is good alone; together it is the most used crown verb. Do not add more. |
| "Raise it for 1.2 s; the first blow that lands on it" | Ichor, Sunstone, Death Turned Back | Three duo counters with one shape. Blown Through and The Exile's Grudge show how a counter can differ. |
| A mark spread to others | Glint, Golden Feathers, Prism | Glint keeps the ray, Golden Feathers the wind; Prism moves to the flank (fix 8). |

## The Weak pairs: options and a recommendation

### Wind and Earth (Unsteady + Tremor): the reaction

**What is wrong.** Capsize (Wind and Water) puts an Unsteady, Soaked enemy flat for 1.2 s; Topple puts an Unsteady,
shaken enemy flat for 1.2 s. Same verb, same number, both Wind. The player cannot tell them apart except by the ring
of dust. The pair's own ideas are Logan Stone (Cornwall's rocking stones) and Walking Dune (the barchan), which is
excellent and stays.

**Option A: Logan Stone, made the reaction.** Unsteady meets Tremor, either order; rest 6 s. It does not fall: it
rocks on its feet like a balanced stone for 3 s. Each of your blows in that time rocks it further and breaks balance
harder (the first at 1.25 times, the second at 1.5), and the third throws it down hard: flat for 1.8 s, and its fall
strikes 20 damage, 30 stagger and a Tremor in 3 m. Elites rock and then reel (40 stagger); gods only take the stagger.
Seen from above: the body leaning to one side, then the other, dust puffing from under alternate feet, a rut widening
round it; then the fall and a ring of dust. Steel: the knockdown is earned by three blows inside a window, so it is the
weapon that fells it, where Capsize is given by the afflictions. Shapes: keep **Sandblast** (the dust of the fall
thrown on downwind as a cone that can rock the next); new **Drifted Over** in place of Logan Stone: when it falls the
wind heaps sand over it and it lies buried to the shoulders 1 s longer, and enemies within 2 m wade at 60% speed.

**Option B: Worn to a Face.** The grit in the wind wears stone: a ventifact turns a new face to the wind each time one
is worn flat. Unsteady meets Tremor: for 5 s a band of pale scour shows on the side you struck from. Each blow you land
from a different side than the last (at least 90 degrees round) is critical and lays an Unsteady; three such blows in a
row topple it for 1.2 s. Seen: a scoured band that moves round the body as you circle it. Steel: footwork round the
enemy is the whole reaction.

**Option C: Lee Drift.** Sand heaps in the lee of whatever stands in the wind. Unsteady meets Tremor: a crescent of sand
3 m wide banks on the enemy's far side for 6 s; enemies on it wade at 50%; a heavy blow from your side drives it back
into the drift, buried to the waist and held 1.5 s. Seen: a sharp-crested crescent of sand behind one body. Steel: the
heavy chooses when the drift closes.

**Recommendation: A.** It keeps the rocking stone the Celtic land owns, turns the knockdown into a rhythm of three
blows, and splits cleanly from Capsize's instant fall. B is a good second if the owner wants more circling play.

### Poison and Water (Poisoned + Soaked): the reaction, Shape A, the duo spell

**What is wrong.** Fouled Water's pool makes every bite reach all who stand in it: Rot Roots (Life and Poison) does
exactly that by root (README flags it). Silver Pool and The Hundred Rivers are quicksilver, which is the Metal and
Poison reaction's substance and the First Emperor's mercury, not water. The one thing only these two make, the
fish-poison of the Pacific shore (the seeds scraped on rock by a tide pool, the stunned fish rising to be gathered by
hand), is a shape.

**Option A: Fish Poison, made the reaction.** Soaked meets Poisoned, either order; rest 6 s. The water runs off it
fouled into a slick 3 m across for 5 s: dark water with a yellow-green sheen and rising bubbles. What stands in it is
Soaked. Any enemy in the slick that comes to carry four doses or more floats up stunned: lifted off its feet and held
1.5 s (elites 0.5 s; gods take 30 stagger instead), and the next weapon blow on it is critical and spends two doses for
6 damage each. Seen: bodies lifting belly-up out of a sheen that ripples with every bite. Steel: the float is a window
for the blade, and the doses are what you built to reach it. Shapes: **Rock Pool** (the slick is 1.5 m and stuns at
two doses: one enemy, often) and **The Ebb Takes It** (the slick runs 6 m away from you as a stream 1.5 m wide over 1 s,
stunning by the same rule along its length).

**Option B: Bitter Tide.** The Water land's rule is the tide. Soaked meets Poisoned: a ring of foul water washes out
from it to 3 m and draws back, every 2 s for 6 s. On each flood every dose on it bites once on everything the wash
reaches; on each ebb its doses do not fade. Seen: a dark ring breathing out and in across the floor. Steel: blows
struck on the ebb lay one more dose (the water is drawn into the wound).

**Duo spell, for either: Poison the Lagoon** (opener). Cooldown 12 s. You crush the fish-poison seed on the floor at
the aim point (within 7 m): a slick spreads in rings to 6 m across and stands 6 s, Soaking all in it. Each weapon blow
you land on an enemy in the slick stirs the water: every other enemy in it takes a dose. Any enemy in it that reaches
four doses floats up as Fish Poison does (past its rest, once each). Seen: grey husks scattered, the sheen spreading out
in rings from where they fell. Steel: the blade stirs the poison through the crowd. This replaces The Hundred Rivers;
if the quicksilver channels are loved, they belong to Metal and Poison.

**Recommendation: A with Poison the Lagoon.** It is the one image no other pair has, it reads instantly (bodies
floating up), and it gives the blade a clear window. Drop Silver Pool with The Hundred Rivers.

### Light and Poison (Marked + Poisoned): the reaction and the duo spell

**What is wrong.** Burning Sap ("each critical blow makes its poison bite at once") is Quicksilver ("each weapon blow
makes its poison bite at once") limited to criticals. It also throws away the best fact of its own image: hogweed sap
does nothing in the shade, and the sun's burn comes later. Bitter Lantern marks with its light and doses with its
smoke, two effects in one place.

**Option A: Burning Sap, given the hogweed's delay** (keep the name). Marked meets Poisoned; rest 6 s. The light wakes
the sap, but the burn waits. For 3 s its poison stops biting and its veins light gold-green under the skin, brighter
each second. Each critical blow in those 3 s raises a weal (a swollen yellow bead on the body, three at most: the
Mark's three). At the end every bite it held back lands at once, doubled, and each weal bursts, splashing two doses on
every enemy within 2.5 m. Seen: veins brightening, beads swelling, then one wet yellow burst. Steel: the three
criticals load the weals; the player chooses to stay for the burst or move on. Distinct from Quicksilver because it is
held, then released, not bitten blow by blow. Shapes: keep **The Light Finds Them**; **Blistered** becomes "the weals
burst at each critical rather than at the end".

**Option B: Drawn Venom** (Garuda, the sun's eagle and the serpent's enemy, whose stone, the emerald, is the old
antidote). Marked meets Poisoned: the light draws the venom out of it in a green-gold thread to your blade. It stops
biting, and your next three blows, on anything, are critical and each lays a third of its doses. Seen: a thin green
thread from the body to the edge, an emerald sheen on the steel. Steel: you carry the poison by hand to where it is
wanted.

**Duo spell, for A: Bared to the Sun** (opener). Cooldown 13 s. You tear the shade off a place: a hard-edged shaft of
sun 5 m across falls on the aim point (within 8 m) for 5 s, drawn as dust motes in the air and a bright floor with the
enemies' shadows thrown sharp across it. Every poisoned enemy in it is Marked and starts Burning Sap at once (past its
rest, once each); each weapon blow you land inside raises one more weal on the one struck. Steel: every weal is a blow.
In the Fire land, where shade is a rule, it reads twice.

**Recommendation: A with Bared to the Sun.** It keeps the plant, makes the light a cause and the poison its result,
and moves the pair off Quicksilver. B is strong but passes doses, which Swarm, Running Silver and The Debt Passes also
do.

### Dark and Poison (Cursed + Poisoned): the reaction and the duo spell

**What is wrong.** Brood adds a dose with each bite while Cursed. Dark's own verbs (pay, take it back, the curse that
feeds) do nothing; the result is a number on Poison. The best image of the pair is already in the file: the gu, the
creatures sealed in a dark jar until the last alive is the poison, which is also the Poison land's own enemy rule ("when
one dies, the nearest one absorbs it and grows"). It sits in the Swarm shape. Built Wasting Grip is a stream of rot
that touches neither.

**Option A: The Sealed Jar.** Cursed meets Poisoned; rest 8 s. A ring of black lacquer 4 m across closes on the floor
round it for 8 s: the jar's mouth, seen from above. Its doses stop fading. Each other poisoned enemy that dies inside
the ring crawls to it as a black swarm and gives it every dose (to twelve, past the usual eight), and each kill you make
inside feeds you 4. When it dies, or the 8 s end, the jar breaks: 5 a dose in 3 m, and half its doses laid on all it
reaches. Seen: a black ring, swarms crawling across the floor to one body, green-black bubbles swelling on it. Steel:
kill order is the play: finish the small ones beside the jar, then break the jar with a finisher. Shapes: **Swarm**
moves on (the broken jar's swarm goes to the nearest enemy within 8 m and seals a new jar there); **Rot to the Bone**
stays.

**Option B: Blue Throat** (the halahala, which Shiva held in his throat until it turned blue). Cursed meets Poisoned:
the dark gathers the poison to its throat as a blue-black knot; its doses stop biting and each bite it would have made
becomes another dose (to twelve). A finisher, or a heavy blow that staggers it, makes it swallow: every dose bites at
once, and a quarter of that damage heals you. Left 6 s, the knot bursts by itself at half. Seen: a dark knot swelling
at the neck. Steel: the heavy picks the moment.

**Duo spell: re-aim Wasting Grip.** The stream seals a jar on the first enemy it bites, past the rest, and each second
of the stream adds a dose to whatever is inside the ring.

**Recommendation: A.** It is the gu exactly, it makes Dark's feeding a reward for the kill, and it turns "who dies
first" into a choice. B is good, but it is a held-then-released bite, which Light and Poison takes if fix 3 is adopted.
Note: Poison's Heresy is named The Last One in the Jar; The Sealed Jar is the pair's own and different in kind, but if
the owner wants no echo at all, call it **Shut in the Dark**.

### Earth and Fire (Tremor + Burn): the reaction and Shape A

**What is wrong.** Baked Earth is "a star of glowing seams 3 m round it", which is Earthlight's star of glowing seams
in orange; Running Seam is lava, which Earth's evolution The Giant's Road already uses; Fired Clay (held, the next
heavy breaks it like a pot) is Turned to Stone. Vitrified Wall, the fort whose stones ran to glass, is the pair's own
and stays.

**Option A: Burnt Mound.** In Bronze Age Ireland and Britain stones were heated in a fire and dropped in water, and
they cracked apart; nearly six thousand mounds of fire-cracked stone are left. Tremor meets Burn; rest 6 s. The shaken
floor throws up three stones round it, 1.5 m out, and its fire heats them red. They lie 6 s, black with orange cracks
and smoking, and burn what touches them (3 damage and a Burn each half second). Any weapon swing that passes over a
stone, or a heavy blow within 1.5 m of one, knocks it along the swing: it skips 7 m, strikes the first enemy for 14
damage, 20 stagger, a Burn and a Tremor, and cracks into hot sherds (6 damage and a Burn in 1.5 m). Seen: three glowing
stones; a skipping stone leaving a line of sparks. Steel: the reaction is ammunition and only the weapon fires it, so a
player weaves light blows to bat each stone at a target. No other pair leaves things on the floor for the blade to
strike. Shapes: **Heaped Stones** (the three fuse into one boulder that a heavy blow rolls 8 m through a line: 20
damage, two Burn and two Tremor each) and **Fire-Cracked** (no batting: each stone cracks by itself when an enemy steps
within 1 m: 12 damage and a Burn in 2 m, a field to drive them across).

**Option B: Fired Clay, made distinct.** Tremor meets Burn: over 1.5 s its skin dulls to clay red, then glazes glossy.
Glazed for 5 s, it does not flinch from light blows (it is hard), but each heavy blow breaks the shell and throws hot
sherds 3 m round (12 damage, a Burn, a Tremor), up to three, the body visibly more holed each time. No hold, so it is not
Turned to Stone: it tells the player "heavies now" and turns the heavy into a crowd hit.

**Option C: Glassed Ground.** The shaken floor under it fuses to black glass 3 m across for 5 s: enemies on it slide
1 m past where they meant to stop, and a heavy blow on an enemy standing on it shatters the floor: 18 damage, 30
stagger and a Burn to all on it. Close to Fire and Lightning's Fulgurite, which is why it is third.

**Recommendation: A.** It is a true meeting of the two (stone made into fire by fire), it is unlike anything else in
the 55, and it is the strongest steel reward in the set: the reaction does nothing until the weapon swings.

### Dark and Ice (Cursed + Chill): the reaction

**What is wrong.** Frostbite makes Chill stop fading and deepen by itself. That is all Ice; Dark is a switch. It is a
quiet number on the pair with two of the richest myths (the sun eaten and the cold that follows; Hel, half living and
half dead), and Swallowed Warmth, the shape where Dark actually takes something, shows the better idea.

**Option A: Stolen Warmth.** Cursed meets Chill; rest 6 s. The curse takes its warmth, and the cold is what is left.
While both last its Chill does not fade, and each of your weapon blows on it draws warmth out of it: one Chill on it,
and 2 health to you (8 a second at most). When it freezes, the last of its warmth is torn out at once: you heal 2 for
every Chill it carried, and it stays frozen 1 s longer. Gods give half. Seen: frost thickening on the body while a
pale wisp of breath-smoke runs from it to you at each blow; at the freeze, one last cloud of breath torn out of it.
Steel: every blow is a draw, so the healing is paced by the weapon. Dark's verb (take it back) and Ice's substance
(cold) become one act. Shapes: keep **Cold Shade**; **The Long Night** in place of Swallowed Warmth: the warmth goes to
the dark instead of to you, and every other enemy within 3 m gains a Chill each second while it is frozen.

**Option B: Sun Eaten.** In a total eclipse the air drops several degrees in minutes. Cursed meets Chill: a disc of
shadow 3.5 m across slides over it and follows it for 4 s; every enemy inside gains a Chill a second and its Chill does
not fade. A heavy blow that shatters a frozen enemy inside ends the shadow in a ring of frost (20 damage, two Chill in
3 m). Close to Light and Dark's disc of dark, which is why it is second.

**Option C: Dead Side** (Hel). Cursed meets Chill: the side of it away from you goes to black ice for 5 s; blows on the
dead side are critical and lay two Chill. Readable, but side-of-blow is Rime's and the Mock Suns', so it is third.

**Recommendation: A.** It is the only option where the dark does something of its own, it is readable as a thread of
breath, and it makes Ice in the Blood (four blows that each draw a vein) read as the same idea. Keep Three Winters.

### Life and Dark (Seeded + Cursed): the reaction

**What is wrong.** Grave Roots holds it 1.2 s and each blow mends you 2: Life's roots and Dark's feeding side by side.
It is also the fourth root hold (Taking Root, Late Frost, Held Down). The strangler fig, which climbs its host, girdles
it, and leaves a hollow lattice trunk standing where the host was, is the real meeting of growth and death, and it is
a shape (Hollow Trunk).

**Option A: Girdled.** Seeded meets Cursed; rest 6 s. Black fig roots wind up it from the floor: one coil. It is not
held: it is slowed 25% and cannot dodge or leap. Each of your weapon blows on it draws the roots one coil tighter (four
at most, rings climbing the body); each coil makes it take 6% more from your weapon and mends you 1 a blow. At four
coils it is girdled: held 1.5 s while the roots crush (20 damage, 40 stagger), and a fifth of what it loses heals you.
If it dies girdled, the roots stand where it stood for 8 s as a hollow trunk: it blocks movement and shots, and Seeds
and Curses what touches it. Seen: dark rings tightening up the body; then a lattice column with nothing inside. Steel:
the blade tightens the coils, and where you make the kill places the wall. Shapes: keep **Corpse Flowers**; new **Fig in
the Crown** in place of Hollow Trunk (now the base): when the trunk forms, its seed drops onto the nearest enemy within
5 m, which starts at one coil.

**Option B: Carrion Bloom.** The corpse plants grow in the dark on what is dying, and call the flies. Seeded meets
Cursed: a pale waxy bloom opens on it; for 4 s every other enemy within 7 m is drawn toward it at 1 m a second, and each
that comes within 2 m is Seeded and Cursed. When the host dies the bloom roots where it fell: a grown thing that mends
you 2 a second while you fight beside it. Steel: it gathers the room onto one point for a sweep.

**Option C: Yew Grave.** A Seeded, Cursed enemy that dies is held in the floor by yew roots, and a black yew stands
there 10 s, a grown thing that Curses and Seeds what comes within 3 m. Only fires on a death, so it does little in the
fight; third.

**Recommendation: A.** One substance doing one thing (a fig that grows tighter on the dying), a steel count you can
see, and a wall placed by the kill. It stops being a hold until the blade earns it.

## OK pairs that could be much better

### Fire and Lightning: the reaction

Flashover spends both at half in one crack: two set-offs added.

- **A. Flashover, re-aimed on flame that conducts.** Flame is ionised and carries current; it is why wildfires trip
  power lines (the engineers' word for it is flashover). Burn meets Static: the fire on it stands up violet at the root
  and goes live for 4 s. Its own Static discharges at once through the flame (7 a stack in 3 m, 20 stagger) and none of
  the Burn is spent. For the 4 s every discharge or arc within 6 m bends into its flame and bursts there again (5 a
  Burn stack in 2.5 m, once every 0.8 s). Seen: arcs bending sideways across the floor into one burning body. Steel:
  heavy blows on other charged enemies throw their charge into the flame.
- **B. Corona as the reaction**, Flashover as a shape. St Elmo's fire crawls over it and arcs leap to neighbours with
  both afflictions. Simple, but arcs to the nearest are already Live Iron's and Lightning's charged blade.
- **Recommendation: A.** A real fact, one image, and it makes the burning enemy the room's conductor. If adopted,
  Metal and Lightning's Draws the Bolt shape (which also pulls every discharge into one body) is replaced; see below.
  Draw Ball Lightning as violet-cored plasma with flame on its skin, not "white fire".

### Water and Life: the duo spell

The built Strangling Vine raises a vine under the nearest enemy and throws it; there is no water in it and it never
touches the flower.

- **A. Re-aim Strangling Vine.** It rises under the nearest Soaked enemy and drinks: the enemy's Soaked is spent and
  Quickening opens on it at once, past the rest; a heavy blow on it while it is lifted bursts the flower for twice the
  heal.
- **B. The Well Overflows** (the Celtic holy wells): a spring opens 3 m across for 6 s; what stands in it is Soaked each
  second, and every Seeded enemy in it Quickens. A zone that lays both, so second.
- **Recommendation: A.** The built spell stays, and now it is the reaction made large.

### Earth and Metal: the reaction

- **A. Anvil Stone, made the reaction.** Tremor meets Sundered: an iron-faced stone heaves up at its back, away from you,
  for 5 s. Your next heavy blow drives it onto the anvil: its Sundered goes to full and the ring of sparks runs out 3 m
  (10 damage, 15 stagger, a notch on each). The ring becomes the heavy blow's payoff. Shapes: **Ringing Iron** (the ring
  at once, no stone) and **Hammer Scale** stay as the two choices.
- **B.** Keep Ringing Iron and add the anvil's rule to the plain reaction (a heavy on a ringing enemy rings it again).
- **Recommendation: A.** Hammer on anvil is the forge; the giant's stones as the smith's anvil is the myth meeting.

### Water and Metal: the reaction and the duo spell

- **Reaction:** give Rust its end. At five notches a plate rusts through and falls to the floor (visible, a wreck): for
  6 s it takes weapon blows as if notched five deep, wet or dry. This is The Plate Falls folded into the reaction; the
  freed Major can be Salt Edge's twin for the guard.
- **Duo spell A: The Sea Takes It** (breaker, 13 s). A wave of brine 6 m wide rolls 7 m; every notched enemy it crosses
  rusts to five notches at once and its joints seize (turns 40% slower) for 4 s; a heavy blow on a seized enemy knocks a
  plate off (20 damage, 35 stagger). Seen: grey-green surf, then orange bloom racing over every plate it touched.
- **Duo spell B:** keep The Weighted Net, but let the water work: each second under the net lays a notch, and hauling
  it with a heavy knocks a plate off every rusted enemy beneath.
- **Recommendation: A.** The Smith's Net (Metal and Dark) and the Golden Chair already net enemies; this pair's crown
  should be rust, its own substance, made large.

### Life and Light: the duo spell

- **A. Turned to the Sun.** Green things turn toward the light. You plant a lit herb at the aim point (within 7 m) for
  6 s; every 2 s it flares, and every Seeded or Marked enemy within 6 m turns to face it for 1 s (a swing already begun
  is finished). Blows on an enemy's back while it faces the herb are critical and Seed it. Elites turn for 0.4 s; gods
  do not turn. Seen: a sprig whose veins glow like lit sap, and a ring of bodies turning toward it at each flare.
  Steel: it hands the blade every back in the room.
- **B. Trim Life-Herb.** Only critical blows (the Mark's) open leaves, and the herb Marks again at every third leaf, so it
  is not every blow critical for 8 s.
- **Recommendation: A.** It changes what the player does (get behind them), it is unlike any other spell, and it leaves
  Red Harvest as Life's alone.

### Poison and Wind: Shape A and the reaction's weight

- Replace **Turned Back** (the body bursts where it lands: Thunder Gust in poison) with **Dust on the Blade**: the shove
  that throws it coats your edge with the yellow grit, and your next three light blows each lay a dose and an Unsteady.
- Give the plain trail more weight: it sheds all its loose doses at once, not one, in a plume that settles over 3 s.
- **Recommendation:** both. The Yellow Gale stays as it is.

### Ice and Metal: the reaction and Shape A

- **Reaction:** move Cold Short's payoff onto the armour, so it stops being Thermal Shock again. The heavy blow on a
  brittle enemy cracks its plates off: its notches stay for the fight, and for 6 s it takes 25% more from weapon blows,
  with no second burst of damage.
- **Shape A:** replace **Spall** (Rime's far-side cone) with **Shed Plate**: the cracked plate falls as a sheet of black
  ice 2 m across under it for 4 s; enemies that step on it slip flat for 0.6 s.
- **Recommendation:** both. Cold Chisel and Snapped Blade are untouched and are the pair's heart.

### Ice and Light: the reaction

- **A. Mock Suns as the reaction**, the fan of rays as a shape. Two mock suns in flat plates of ice hang either side of
  it for 4 s and turn with it; a blow from beside one is critical, lays two Chill and does not spend the Mark. The
  flank is a fresh verb no other pair asks for.
- **B. Lens of Ice as the reaction.** While it is frozen its Mark is not spent, and its shatter throws the Mark round.
- **Recommendation: A.** It is the most readable from above (two glints beside the body) and takes Prism off the
  far-side cone.

### Metal and Lightning: the reaction and Shape B

- **Reaction:** Live Iron arcs **only from iron to iron**: an arc goes to the nearest Sundered enemy within 8 m, never to
  a clean one, and lays a notch where it lands. The notches become a wire the player lays with heavy blows, which
  Lightning's charged blade (any second enemy) never asks for.
- **Shape B:** replace Draws the Bolt with **Forged Bolt** (the Cyclopes, named Thunder, Lightning and Bright, forged the
  bolt): each weapon blow on a Live Iron enemy hammers one of its Static into a glowing iron spike standing in it (three
  at most); a heavy blow tears them out as one thunderbolt thrown in a line 10 m: 12 damage a spike, 20 stagger, two
  Static to all on the line.
- **Recommendation:** both. Drawn Iron can stay as Shape A; the Lightning Rod's Lodestone pulls to a rod, this to a body.

### Shorter notes on the other OK pairs

- **Life and Earth:** make Split Rock the plain reaction (held 2 s, and a heavy splits the floor round it); Root Web
  and a new short hold become the shapes.
- **Lightning and Life:** with no grown thing near, let the fern itself be the plant: it throws one bolt to the nearest
  Seeded enemy, so the reaction never falls to a 10-damage crack.
- **Earth and Poison:** make Chokedamp the plain reaction (the damp runs along the floor to the other shaken), so it
  stops being Miasma on the ground.
- **Metal and Fire:** if the three bronze pours must be cut to two, Tipped Crucible is the one that is a crown; keep it,
  and let the Hammer's Quench be the one that changes.
- **Poison and Ice:** Rime Over Rime is the thinnest of the lay-both crowns. Its heavy-blow shatter of every ring is the
  part to grow.
- **Water and Dark:** fine as it stands; the spell carries it.
- **Light and Wind:** fine as an opener pair; Bane of Serpents is the hit and could be the plain reaction's.
